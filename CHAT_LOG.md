Condensed record of the working chat (15–24 Nov 2026). Numbers are from the result files in this
repo unless marked *estimate* or *analytic*.

## 1. Removing a large copper island from FR4 (Fnirsi mini hot plate)

- Heat alone does not lift copper: the bond softens around 200–250 °C, but you still need an
  edge to start from and a slow pull.
- Method: score the outline with a scalpel, cut the area into 3–5 mm strips, lift a corner,
  preheat to ~150–200 °C and use **hot air directly on the copper** (~300–350 °C) while pulling at
  a shallow angle. Copper on top of 1.6 mm FR4 heated from below stays much colder than the plate setpoint.
- Do not exceed ~280 °C on FR4 (browning, brominated fumes).
- **Near a patch antenna**: etch instead of peeling (Kapton/marker mask, sodium persulfate or ferric
  chloride), or peel only beyond ~5–10 mm and etch the ring next to the patch, so the radiating
  edges are not lifted or distorted.
- No dedicated "peeling machine" exists. Options: bubble etch tank (best: CNC isolation +
  Kapton mask cut along the groove + etch), hot-air rework station, a **CNC cross-hatch grid
  (5–10 mm pitch) in the waste copper** so it peels in small squares, or a fiber laser.
- Leftover copper (even floating squares) near a patch detunes it, so all of it has to come off.

## 2. HFSS: effect of top-layer copper around a 2.45 GHz patch (0.8 mm FR4, 100 x 75 mm)

Scripts: `01_fr4_patch_0p8mm_hfss/` (PyAEDT 1.10, AEDT 2025.1, non-graphical).
PyAEDT 1.x API fix: `insert_infinite_sphere(theta_*, phi_*)` instead of `x_*, y_*`.

| Run | Feed | Patch L / inset | Pour gap | Bare f0 | Copper f0 | Shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coax probe | 29.05 / – | 10 mm | results overwritten | – | – |
| 2 | Inset microstrip | 29.05 / 8.92 | 5 mm | 2.249 GHz, -11.3 dB, BW 15 MHz | 2.344 GHz, -12.0 dB, BW 19 MHz | **+95 MHz** |
| 3 | Inset microstrip | 26.67 / 6.5 | 10 mm | 2.617 GHz, -20.6 dB, BW 42 MHz | 2.613 GHz, -21.3 dB, BW 43 MHz | **-4 MHz** |

- Conclusion: at 5 mm the pour detunes the patch by several bandwidths; at 10 mm the effect is
  ~10 % of the bandwidth (negligible).
- The transmission-line model was ~8 % off on 0.8 mm FR4 (29.05 mm gave 2.249 GHz). Interpolating
  runs 2 and 3 suggests **L ≈ 28.0 mm, inset ≈ 6.8 mm** for 2.45 GHz — *not yet simulated*.
- Gain values in the summary are at 2.45 GHz, i.e. off resonance in both runs — ignore them.
- Gerbers for EasyTrace5000 (`cnc/`, generated by `make_cnc.py` from the same parameters):
  top copper with 10 mm pour, top copper patch-only, board outline. **The current files are for
  L = 26.67 mm (2.617 GHz): regenerate after retuning.**

## 4. 1x2 array, 2.45 GHz, 1.6 mm FR4, 160 x 100 mm, no pour

**First version (legacy, HFSS):** edge-fed, lambda/4 transformer from R_edge (Balanis, 322 ohm) to
50 ohm → 127 ohm line = 0.34 mm wide. Too thin to cut by hand; HFSS auto-tune run never completed.

**Final version (openEMS):** inset-fed patches matched to 50 ohm, 50 ohm lines to a T, 35.36 ohm
lambda/4 (5.22 x 16.38 mm) back to 50 ohm. Narrowest feature 1.5 mm (inset notch).
openEMS was built from source (Linux) because neither apt nor conda-forge had it.

Element tuning (80 x 100 mm half board, 0.6 mm mesh):

| L / inset (mm) | f0 | S11 |
|---|---|---|
| 28.81 / 8.85 (analytic) | 2.421 GHz | -26.2 dB |
| 28.47 / 8.75 | 2.446 GHz | -25.4 dB, BW 61 MHz |
| same, 0.4 mm mesh | 2.454 GHz | -24.1 dB (+8 MHz → mesh converged) |

Full array:

| L / inset | f0 | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 28.47 / 8.75 | 2.426 GHz, -29.3 dB | coupling + T junction pull f0 down 20 MHz |
| **28.19 / 8.66** | **2.449 GHz, -29.4 dB** | -10 dB 2.411–2.486 GHz; S11 at 2.400/2.437/2.45/2.462/2.4835 = -8.1/-19.5/-28.9/-18.2/-10.7 dB |

Far field: Dmax 10.2 dBi, radiation efficiency 41 % (FR4 tan d 0.02 + feed), gain 6.3 dBi.
**The beam tilts ~30° toward the feed side in the E-plane**; broadside gain ≈ 3.4 dBi (likely
feed-line radiation, not verified).

Bugs found and fixed along the way: tuning rule used a spurious Re(Z) peak; NF2FF `center` is in
**metres** and `read_cached=True` is the default — both gave negative radiated power until fixed.

**Measured (built board): resonance 2.84–2.94 GHz, ~18 % high. Open issue.** Candidates: print
scaled ("Fit to page" → L ≈ 24 mm), substrate εr ≈ 3.1 instead of 4.4 (the wider measured band
supports this), non-solid ground (tape seams), VNA calibration. Needed: measured patch L/W and the
board material, then re-fit εr from the measured band.

## 5. 1x2 array, 5.8 GHz, 1.6 mm FR4, 100 x 75 mm

Element tuning (36 x 75 mm half board, 0.4 mm mesh): L 11.74 → 11.63 → **11.60 mm**, inset 3.58 mm,
-44 dB at 5.796 GHz, BW 245 MHz. W 15.73, spacing 36 mm, notch 1.0 mm.

Full array:
- Square 90° feed bends: dip moved to 5.98 GHz, **S11 at 5.8 GHz only -8.6 dB** (Zin 32 - j26 ohm).
  A square bend in a 3.06 mm line adds ~0.34 pF ≈ -j80 ohm at 5.8 GHz.
- **Mitered bends** (Douville–James, M ≈ 57 %, 3.48 mm legs): S11 -10.8 / -14.1 / -17.5 dB at
  5.725 / 5.8 / 5.875 GHz, -10 dB band 5.70–6.12 GHz. Dmax 9.6 dBi, efficiency 50 %, gain 6.6 dBi.

Same caveat as §4: if the board really is εr ≈ 3.1, this lands near 6.8 GHz.

## 6. Antennas on a single sheet of A4 copy paper

Patch on paper — *estimates* (Q-based, εr 3, tan d 0.06):

| Substrate | Efficiency 2.45 / 5.8 GHz | 50 ohm microstrip |
|---|---|---|
| 1 sheet, 0.1 mm, patch + full copper back | ~2.5 % / ~6 % (≈ -10 / -6 dBi) | 0.25 mm wide |
| ~16 sheets, 1.6 mm | ~33 % / ~54 % | ~4 mm |
| Paper carrier over 3–5 mm air | ~80 %+ | — |

The uploaded paper ("Printing antenna on paper: a cost-effective method") reports a patch at
εr 2 / 0.25 mm with 7.7 dBi **simulated only**; measured resonance 2.68 GHz vs 2.42 simulated,
1.11 % bandwidth.

**Chosen approach: uniplanar CPW-fed slotted monopole** (after Nair, Ray, Swaminathan,
arXiv:2202.03266, photo paper 0.22 mm, εr 3.2), copper tape on one side only, no back ground.
CPW gap widened from 0.2 to 0.5 mm so it can be cut with a scalpel (on 0.1 mm paper the CPW is
~70–83 ohm; acceptable for a short feed).

openEMS on copy paper (0.1 mm, εr 3.0, tan d 0.06), two 100 ohm gap ports in parallel:

| Tag | Change | Low band | High band |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | paper geometry, s = 0.5 | 2.53–3.22 GHz | 5.38–5.97 GHz |
| **C** | **radiator height 24 mm** | **2.35–2.97 GHz** (2.4 GHz WiFi) | 4.89–5.49 GHz |
| D | radiator width 27 mm | 2.47–3.22 GHz | 5.26–5.41 GHz |
| E | C scaled x0.4517 | none (-9.8 dB, R ≈ 26 ohm) | — |
| F | E + ground gap 2.0 mm | — | 4.99–5.85 GHz |
| **G** | **scale 0.42, gap 2.0** | — | **5.16–6.04 GHz** (5.8 GHz WiFi) |

Both bands move together with pattern size, so the 2.4 and 5.8 GHz antennas were made separate.
Final: **C** (50.0 x 46.6 mm, 2.0 dBi, 94 %) and **G** (22.6 x 21.6 mm, 2.6 dBi). Efficiency
values slightly above 100 % at 5.8 GHz are FDTD port/NF2FF error (~5 %).
Variant A (0.2 mm gap) was computed before a port-combination bug fix and is omitted.

Real-life risks: coax common-mode current on the small grounds (use a ferrite clip), copy-paper εr
spread (2.5–3.5), hand-cut 0.5 mm gaps, non-conductive tape glue at seams, SMA not modelled.
